Privacy

What happens to your WhatsApp chat when you play Who is bro ?

  • The chat is read on the host's phone and never sent to a server.
  • Nothing is kept anywhere but on the host's phone, and only for the length of the game.
  • Only players accepted by the host receive anything, and only what they need to play.
  • No account, no cookies, no ads, no visitor tracking.

The imported chat

When the host imports the WhatsApp export (a .txt or .zip file), the game reads and analyzes it in the page, on their phone. The file is never sent to any server: not ours (we don't have one), not anyone else's.

So that an accidental page reload doesn't end the game, the host's phone keeps the game in progress in the browser (the imported file, the analysis and the scores), on that phone only. This copy is only used to resume the game: it's deleted the next time the site is opened once the game's tab has been closed (and in any case once it's more than 3 hours old). The exported file itself stays wherever WhatsApp saved it (in Files on iPhone): you can delete it after the game.

What the other players receive

The players' phones connect directly to the host's phone. They only receive:

  • the list of group members (names as saved in WhatsApp) and their message counts, so everyone can pick who they are;
  • each round, the thing to guess: a phrase, an emoji, a title ("the ghost of the group"…), a short real message, a sticker or a GIF;
  • at the reveal, the answer, an example message and a few numbers;
  • with a media export: the stickers and GIFs used in rounds, the photos and stickers in the end montage and slideshow, and photos in rounds. The host can turn photos off before starting the game.

The rest of the chat never leaves the host's phone. As with anything shown on a screen, a player could take a screenshot: only play with people you trust.

Who can join a game

  • Each game has a 6-letter code (191 million possibilities), impossible to guess at random.
  • A new phone enters a first name and waits for the host to accept it. Until then, it receives nothing: no names, no messages, no photos. A decline is final for that game.
  • Once the game has started, no one new can join. Only players already accepted can come back after losing connection.

Tip: only share the link or QR code with the players, not in a bigger group.

How the phones talk to each other

The players' phones talk directly to each other, without going through a game server. The exchanges are encrypted: no one else can read them.

A public service is only used to put the phones in touch at the start, a bit like a switchboard: it never sees what's said during the game.

What's kept on your phone

So you can get back into the game after a reload, the page keeps a random ID and the first name you typed to join, in that browser tab. They're deleted when you close the tab. If you change the game's language, that choice is remembered on your phone. No cookies.

The "Scan the QR code" button only uses the camera to read the code, on your phone: no image is sent or kept, and the camera turns off as soon as the code is read.
